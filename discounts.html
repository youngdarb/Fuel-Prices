Fuel discount cards and loyalty schemes in Guernsey

Which cards save you money, which forecourts accept them, and how to work out whether a discount actually beats a cheaper price down the road.

Almost every fuel group on the island runs some kind of discount, but they work in completely different ways. Some take money off at the pump, some only apply on certain days, and a couple are prize draws rather than a saving at all.

This page lists what is available, then shows the simple sum for deciding whether it is worth driving to a particular forecourt. Prices change most weeks, so check the live price table before you go.

Does the discount actually beat the cheaper forecourt?

This is where people lose money without realising. A 4p card sounds generous, but if that forecourt is already 5p dearer than the cheapest on the island, you are still paying more.

The sum is simple. Take the pump price, subtract your discount, and compare that against the cheapest price on the live table.

Worked example

Say the cheapest unleaded on the island is 168.9, and your nearest Rubis forecourt is showing 172.9 with a 4p card.

172.9 minus 4p is 168.9. Exactly the same. At that point the card has only cancelled out the difference, so you may as well use whichever is closer.

If the Rubis price were 171.9 instead, the card would put you at 167.9, which is a genuine 1p per litre better than the cheapest headline price.

On a 50 litre fill, 1p per litre is 50p. Over a year of weekly fills that is around £26, so it is worth knowing but it is not life changing. The bigger savings come from avoiding the most expensive forecourts entirely, where the spread across the island can be 7p or more per litre.

What the spread is worth

If the cheapest and dearest forecourts are 7p apart, a 50 litre fill costs £3.50 more at the wrong one. Fill up weekly and that is roughly £180 a year, for fuel that came off the same boat.

Timing your fill

Know the update days

CIFL sites tend to change prices on Mondays and Tuesdays, and Rubis sites on Fridays. If prices are rising, filling before those days can save you a little. If they are falling, waiting can. Neither is a guarantee, but it explains why the island does not all move at once.

Use the ATF discount days

The ATF offer is one of the easier wins because it needs no card at all. If an ATF forecourt is already competitive on the live table, filling on a Wednesday or Sunday takes another 2p per litre off.

Do not drive across the island for it

Guernsey is small, but fuel spent getting somewhere still counts. A ten minute detour to save 2p on a 50 litre fill saves you a pound. If it is on your way, take it. If it is not, it rarely pays.

Other ways to spend less

  • Check super unleaded is worth it. Most cars run perfectly well on standard 95 RON. Unless your handbook asks for higher octane, super is usually money spent for nothing.
  • Watch for bulk and account rates. If you run a boat or a business, GPD and Boatworks+ both discount on volume, generally from 1,000 litres.
  • Heating oil is a bigger lever than petrol. A single tank of heating oil dwarfs a fill of petrol, so comparing supplier quotes is usually worth more than any pump discount. Quotes are listed on the main page.
  • Keep tyres at the right pressure. Underinflated tyres measurably increase consumption, and it costs nothing to check.
